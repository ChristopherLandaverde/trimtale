# Go-To-Market Timing

## Positioning

Avoid positioning the product as only:

> AI for GTM

That sounds generic and easy to dismiss.

Sharper positioning:

> GTM and GA4 observability for agencies.

Plain-language promise:

> Know when client tracking breaks before reporting day.

The agent UX is useful for discovery and debugging, but the recurring weekly scan is what makes the product sticky.

## When to build the website

### Build a simple waitlist page now

Build it before the full product is ready, but keep it honest.

The page should sell the problem and collect conversations:

- Agencies lose trust when client tracking breaks silently.
- GTM Preview helps during setup, but it does not monitor after handoff.
- Website, campaign, consent, and GTM changes break events without warning.
- GTM Observability runs evidence-backed checks and sends a weekly tracking health report.

Call to action:

> Join the agency pilot.

Do not overbuild the marketing site yet. One strong landing page, one demo GIF/video, and one intake form are enough.

### Build a stronger website after Stage 2

Once saved tracking specs exist, the site can show the real workflow:

```text
Define critical events
  -> verify browser evidence
  -> save baseline
  -> run weekly scan
  -> get report
```

At this point, the site should include screenshots, a sample health report, and a clear agency use case.

### Build a sales-ready website after Stage 4

Once weekly scans work, the website can sell a real product:

- Client dashboard.
- Weekly tracking health report.
- Failure examples.
- Evidence screenshots.
- Pricing.
- Pilot onboarding.

## When to start marketing

### Start founder-led discovery now

Do not wait for the agent.

Talk to:

- Small analytics agencies.
- PPC agencies.
- Webflow/Shopify/WordPress agencies that own tracking.
- Freelance GA4/GTM consultants.

Questions:

- How do you know tracking broke?
- How often do clients change sites without telling you?
- What do you check before reporting day?
- How do you prove tracking worked after a fix?
- Would a weekly health report save time or reduce client risk?

### Start public building after Stage 1

Once the navigation MVP verifies real browser evidence, share:

- Short demos.
- "I reduced nav click setup from manual GTM work to guided verification."
- Debugging clips showing pass/fail evidence.

This is good for LinkedIn, X, Indie Hackers, and analytics communities.

### Start agency pilots after Stage 3

The right pilot moment is when the product can diagnose failures, not just generate GTM JSON.

Pilot offer:

> I will monitor 3 critical events on 3 client sites and send a weekly tracking health report.

The pilot does not need a polished website. It needs reliable evidence and useful reports.

### Start paid selling after Stage 4

Charge when weekly scans produce recurring value.

Early pricing ideas:

- Per monitored site.
- Per client workspace.
- Agency bundle for 5-10 client sites.

Do not price around "AI usage." Price around avoided reporting risk and saved QA labor.

## Message hierarchy

Primary message:

> Know when client tracking breaks before reporting day.

Secondary messages:

- Weekly GTM and GA4 tracking health checks.
- Evidence-backed debugging for broken events.
- Browser, GTM, and GA4 signals in one place.
- Draft fixes with human approval.

Avoid early claims:

- Fully automated publishing.
- Guaranteed Google Ads attribution confirmation.
- Replacing GTM.
- Replacing analytics experts.

## First niche

Start with agencies that manage lead-generation sites.

Reasons:

- Navigation clicks, CTA clicks, and lead forms are repeatable.
- Broken tracking creates client trust issues.
- Weekly reporting cadence makes the pain obvious.
- Google Ads and GA4 quality matters to revenue conversations.

Do not start with ecommerce, server-side tagging, or complex consent deployments unless a pilot customer pulls you there.
